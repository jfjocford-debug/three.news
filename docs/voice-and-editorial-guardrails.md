# Voice & Editorial Guardrails — V4

**Changelog from V3:** Added the Tribute Register — a third voice register for natural death, terminal illness, and grief-tribute stories, distinct from both full voice and the strict plain register. Added a mandatory human-review category for named individuals acquitted or cleared in sexual-abuse-adjacent cases, regardless of which register the story is written in. Both surfaced from the first real Select-stage test run against live news data, not from hypothetical discussion.

**Changelog from V2:** Added the Vibe vs. Fabrication distinction as a governing principle — interpretive/metaphorical/narrative framing is now fully permitted, while invented facts, quotes, or events remain absolutely forbidden regardless of framing. Formalized the Humor Toolkit (real jokes, one per story, restricted to Takeaway or the closing beat of What Happened, never announced; understatement as a separate, placement-free tool). Resolved the Core Identity section's contradiction with the humor rules established in testing.

This is the single reference doc for how every story gets written. It consolidates everything decided across the content-template, voice-testing, and cultural-fluency revamp process, including the exception rule that governs when the house voice shifts to plain delivery.

---

## 1. The Content Structure (all stories, every register)

Every story uses the same six fields, regardless of which voice register applies to it.

| Field | Length | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Title Case. Must fit one line on a standard mobile screen without wrapping (2026 screen sizes). | States a fact. No questions. Superlatives allowed only if verifiable (see §3). |
| What Happened | 2-3 sentences, 40-60 words | Who/what/when/where. Adjectives allowed only if factual (see §3) — never adjectives that imply judgment. |
| Why It Matters | 2-4 sentences, **40-80 words (up to 100 only if the point genuinely needs it)** | Three jobs now, not one: states impact, notes discourse shape without taking a side, and lands a pattern-level insight. Full rules in §5. |
| What's Next | 1 sentence, 15-25 words | Grounded in a real, stated next step. If none exists, say so plainly. |
| Artwork | — | Renaissance, Classical Greek, Neoclassicism (Baroque case-by-case). Internal-only rationale sentence required — documents the metaphor logic, never shown to reader. |
| Takeaway | 1 sentence, 12-20 words | Must stand alone with zero context — this is the field people screenshot. |

Total target has grown from the original 120-160 words, since Why It Matters now carries more weight — treat 150-200 words across all fields as the new working range, not a hard ceiling.

## 2. The House Voice

### Core Identity
Culturally fluent, internet-native, and deadpan — without ever using slang or memes to get there. It should read like something that would naturally surface in a social feed, not a newsroom, and it must have a real point of view: observational, dry, emotionally controlled. Never preachy, never editorialized.

The delivery is deadpan, concise, and slightly surreal in its neutrality — flat enough that a reader might double-take because the tone is so unreactive it becomes unintentionally funny. **The absurdity is implied, never performed.** No sensationalism, no corporate or institutional phrasing. Short, clean, confident sentences. (Jokes and punchlines are conditionally allowed — see the Humor Toolkit below, not a blanket ban.)

The test: it should read like a culturally aware reporter covering the news with the emotional detachment of a weather report.

### Vibe vs. Fabrication — The Line That Actually Matters
This is the single most important distinction in the whole voice system, and it's worth stating as plainly as possible: **interpretive, metaphorical, and narrative language is fully permitted to describe tone, vibe, or cultural meaning. Inventing facts, quotes, or events is never permitted, under any framing.**

- Vibe ≠ fabrication. Metaphor ≠ misinformation. Narrative ≠ falsehood.
- Calling a 41-point blowout "a funeral" or "a public execution" is framing — it describes how a real, verified result *felt*, and doesn't claim anything happened that didn't.
- Inventing a quote, a specific reaction, or a detail that was never reported — even something small and plausible-sounding, like a fake "vibe consultant" hire or a fabricated online comment — is fabrication, full stop, regardless of how well it fits the voice.
- **The concrete test:** "left as a cautionary tale" is vibe — an interpretation of a real result. "Left to a chorus of boos" is a fabricated event dressed as color, unless boos were actually reported. If a detail could be fact-checked and would fail, it's fabrication. If it's describing the feeling of something real and verified, it's framing.
- When a specific number or claim can't be independently verified, it gets left out entirely — never softened into a hedge, never included because it "sounds right." A gap in the reporting is not an invitation to fill it with something plausible.

This permission was tested across a college football blowout, a dry procedural court ruling, and a corporate valuation story — all three held up. The voice can lean as hard into narrative and metaphor as the material allows; it just can never do so by inventing what actually happened.

### Headline Mechanics
- Title Case (a deliberate, considered contrast against the otherwise internet-native voice — not an oversight)
- Simple declarative clause, present tense
- Neutral verbs only: *can, is, wants, hit, wages, makes, gets, announces* — never *slams, blasts, sparks, ignites*
- No punctuation, no flair
- Fits one line on mobile

### Discourse Awareness
The voice can now acknowledge that public reaction exists and roughly what shape it takes — this is new, and it's the piece that makes the voice feel aware of the internet rather than reporting from outside it. The line: **note that the discourse exists, never characterize who's right.** "This drew immediate, widespread reaction online" is in bounds. "The backlash was justified" or "critics rightly pointed out" is not — that's a verdict wearing a discourse-awareness costume.

### Humor Toolkit: Jokes and Understatement
Two tools are available, and which one fits depends on the story — not a hierarchy where one replaces the other.

**Real jokes (setup and landing, not just vivid description) are allowed, with limits:**
- One real joke maximum per story.
- It lives in the **Takeaway, or the closing beat of What Happened** — never in Why It Matters, which stays joke-free always, since that field's job is the pattern-level insight and asking it to also land a punchline weakens both.
- Never signal it's coming. No "funny," "ironically," "the kicker is." State it flat and let the reader notice it themselves — announcing a joke kills deadpan instantly.

**Understatement (stating something severe or absurd as though it were unremarkable, no setup or turn) has no placement restriction** — it's lower-risk than a real joke and can appear anywhere in the piece, including Why It Matters, since it's a register choice rather than a structural punchline.

Both tools stay subordinate to the fabrication rule above — neither licenses inventing anything.

### Where the deadpan voice applies
All stories that do not trigger the exception below. This includes serious-but-not-violent topics — corporate scandal, political conflict, economic hardship, corruption, fraud — those stay in full voice, discourse awareness and philosophical layer included.

## 3. Two Adjustments to the Original Ruleset

**Superlatives:** allowed if factual and verifiable, not allowed if they're a reaction dressed as description.
- Allowed: "largest deal in company history," "first ruling of its kind," "fastest-growing since 2019"
- Not allowed: "shocking," "stunning," "unbelievable," "massive"
- Test: could this word be fact-checked, or is it just telling the reader how to feel?

**What Happened adjectives:** allowed if they add a fact the reader couldn't otherwise picture, not allowed if they're a verdict.
- Allowed: "a decade-long scheme," "a reinforced underground shelter"
- Not allowed: "a troubling scheme," "a devastating shelter"
- Same mechanism-vs-verdict test as Why It Matters, applied one field earlier.

## 4. The Exception: When the Voice Shifts to Plain

**Trigger:** death, violence, or child harm — involving anyone, named or not.

**Narrower than it used to be, on purpose.** The trigger no longer fires just because a story involves an active criminal or legal proceeding with a real named individual — fraud, corruption, scams, and other non-violent crime stay in full voice, philosophical layer included. It fires specifically for death, violence, and child harm, regardless of whether anyone involved is named.

It does **not** cover: corporate investigations, political conflict, economic hardship, fraud, serious-but-non-violent institutional stories (McKinsey under investigation, Cuba's economy, a fake-athlete investment scam), or stories where the danger to children is the thing being *mitigated* rather than *inflicted* (e.g., hospitals moving births underground to protect newborns from strikes) — those stay in full voice. The trigger is for harm being done, not for risk being managed around.

**What the plain register is, when the trigger fires:**
Same six-field structure, same length targets. What changes:
- No absurdist framing or rhythm built for a punchline
- Verbs stay functional, not colorful
- The takeaway states what's true — it does not have a rhetorical turn or a setup/payoff shape
- Still flat and economical — this is not a shift into somber or dramatic language, just a removal of the comedic mechanism

**Why It Matters still gets a philosophical layer here — but "insight stays, ornamentation goes."** A trigger-category story can still carry real, substantive reflection; it just can't be delivered as a crafted, aphoristic, quotable line. The insight should read like plain observation, not like something built to be repeated. See the worked example below (Lindsay Clancy) for the contrast between an ornamented version and the version that actually ships.

**Safeguard:** every story is reviewed by a human editor before publish. The trigger above is the working definition, but editorial judgment is the actual check — the rule exists to make the call fast and consistent, not to replace review.

### Mandatory Human Review — Beyond the Register Question
Some stories need a human's eyes before publish regardless of which register they're written in, because the risk isn't tonal, it's reputational or legal. Three categories identified so far:

1. **A named individual acquitted or cleared of charges in a sexual-abuse-adjacent case.** Even careful, neutral, plain-register language about an acquittal carries real reputational risk to someone the legal system found not guilty — that's not a voice problem the guardrails can write their way around, it's a "does this need a person to actually look at it" problem.
2. **Disputed or contested violence allegations, especially involving law enforcement or an institutional power imbalance.** When accounts conflict — an officer's version versus a civilian's, institutional statements versus video evidence — getting the framing fair and accurate matters more than getting the voice right, and that's a judgment call worth a human's eyes before publish.

Auto-publish does not apply to either category; both hold for review the same way an exception-triggered story does, but for a different reason. This list may grow as more real cases surface.

### The Tribute Register
A third register, distinct from both full voice and the strict plain register — for **natural death, terminal illness, and grief-tribute stories.** This does not replace the exception trigger; it's a more specific register for a subset of death-related stories where the standard plain-register treatment is actively the wrong instinct.

**Applies to:** deaths from age or illness, obituaries and legacy pieces, and stories where a living person is publicly processing grief (dedicating a performance or achievement to someone they lost, a tribute on an anniversary of a death, etc.).

**Does not apply to:** violent death, death tied to an active criminal or legal proceeding, or death that is itself the subject of dispute or wrongdoing — those stay under the strict plain register above. The line: **is the death itself a tragedy to reckon with soberly, or a life being honored?** A murder trial is the former. A drag performer's obituary after five decades of work is the latter.

**What changes in this register:**
- **Warmth, not neutrality, is the goal.** This is the one place the deadpan core identity is deliberately set aside.
- No jokes, no understatement-as-humor. The Humor Toolkit does not apply here at all.
- **Why It Matters is explicitly permitted to be more crafted and resonant than the plain register allows** — this is a deliberate reversal of the Clancy "insight stays, ornamentation goes" rule, not an oversight. Honoring a life well-lived is a fundamentally different act than reflecting on a tragedy; a genuinely moving, even quotable line here is the goal, not a risk to guard against. Lean toward giving the reader something of real value to carry with them — closer to a well-written eulogy than a news brief.
- Facts and quotes still follow the fabrication rule exactly as everywhere else — warmth is a register choice, not license to invent sentiment or reactions that weren't real.
- The swap test (§5) doesn't apply the same way here — a tribute is allowed to be specific to the actual person's life and legacy, not required to generalize into a pattern about death in general.

## 5. Why It Matters: The Philosophical Layer

This field now does three jobs, in order:
1. **States impact** — who's affected, how (unchanged from the original rule).
2. **Notes discourse shape, neutrally** — if relevant, that public reaction exists and roughly its size/shape, never who's right (see §2).
3. **Lands a pattern-level insight** — a genuine idea, not a verdict on the specific case.

### The Swap Test
This is the fast, practical way to check a draft: **would the philosophical line still be true if you swapped in a different company, election, officer, or case?** If yes, it's pattern-level insight — it's saying something about how power, institutions, trust, or human behavior generally work, using this story as the occasion. If the line stops being true the moment you change the specific names involved, it's not insight — it's a verdict about this particular case, dressed up as something bigger.

- **Passes the swap test:** "Valuations like this tend to say more about what a market believes is coming than about what's actually changed inside the company that day." (True for any company hitting a milestone valuation.)
- **Fails the swap test:** "This valuation shows Google's AI strategy is finally paying off." (A verdict specific to Google — stops being true if you swap the company.)

### For Trigger-Category Stories: Insight Stays, Ornamentation Goes
The same swap test applies, but the delivery gets plainer. Compare:
- **Ornamented (do not ship):** *"Justice is not a straight line — it's a negotiation between evidence, interpretation, and the limits of what people can carry."* — Passes the swap test, genuinely insightful, but reaches for a crafted, quotable turn of phrase using a real tragedy as the occasion for it. That's the specific failure mode: polish for its own sake, applied to someone's real grief.
- **Plain (ships):** *"A mistrial doesn't answer the question everyone wanted answered — it just ends the asking, for now."* — Same underlying idea, delivered as observation rather than as a line built to be repeated.

## 6. Worked Examples

### Full voice — Missouri congressional map ruling
**Headline:** Missouri Court Blocks New GOP Map
**What Happened:** The Missouri Supreme Court ruled Thursday that the state's new congressional map cannot be used in the November election. The map already ran in the August primary. Missouri reverts to its previous district lines while voters decide the new map's fate on the ballot.
**Why It Matters:** Republicans hoped the new map would help hold their House majority. Fights like this rarely end with the map itself — they end with whoever controls how maps get challenged next. It's less a story about district lines than about who keeps getting to redraw the rules.
**What's Next:** Missouri's attorney general says she'll appeal directly to the U.S. Supreme Court.
**Takeaway:** Missouri just ends up using its old map after all, at least for now.

### Full voice — Google hits $2 trillion
**Headline:** Google Is Now Worth $2 Trillion
**What Happened:** Google's market valuation crossed $2 trillion this week, driven largely by investor confidence in its AI investments.
**Why It Matters:** The valuation reflects belief in what's coming more than any current earnings shift. Milestones like this tend to say more about what a market believes is coming than about what's actually changed inside the company that day.
**What's Next:** Analysts expect the company's next earnings call to test whether that confidence holds.
**Takeaway:** Google just crossed $2 trillion on a bet about the future, not a report on the present.

### Full voice — cop fired over a balaclava-filter video
**Headline:** Cop Fired For Balaclava Filter
**What Happened:** A police department fired an officer after a video using a balaclava filter circulated online, which the department said crossed a line between humor and department image. The clip drew wide attention across social platforms within days.
**Why It Matters:** Rules about how public employees can present themselves online tend to lag years behind how casually everyone else already treats the same platforms. The gap between what institutions allow and what the internet considers normal is where stories like this keep coming from.
**What's Next:** The officer's union has not said whether it will appeal.
**Takeaway:** The internet moved on from the joke faster than the department did.

### Full voice — fake NFL player investment scam (named-trigger, narrowed)
**Headline:** Man Accused Of Posing As 49ers Player In Scam
**What Happened:** A man is accused of impersonating a 49ers player to solicit money from women who believed they were helping an actual NFL athlete.
**Why It Matters:** If identity is now something you can download, imitate, or assemble from public data, trust becomes a negotiation instead of a baseline. That shift matters because the next scam won't look like a fake athlete — it'll look like someone you already believe.
**What's Next:** Authorities have not said whether additional victims have come forward.
**Takeaway:** The scam wasn't the fake identity — it was how little proof anyone needed to believe it.

*This is a named individual in an active criminal proceeding, but it does not involve violence or child harm — full voice applies, philosophical layer included.*

### Full voice — Ukraine bomb shelter births
**Headline:** Ukraine Delivers Babies In Bomb Shelters
**What Happened:** Hospitals across Ukraine have moved maternity care underground as Russian strikes continue. Staff relocate patients and equipment each time alarms sound. Deliveries continue on the same schedule, several floors below ground.
**Why It Matters:** Families now give birth in reinforced rooms instead of hospital wards. When the systems meant to protect people start reorganizing around a threat instead of removing it, that becomes the new normal — not because anyone accepted it, but because the alternative stopped being available.
**What's Next:** Shelter-based care continues as long as the strikes do.
**Takeaway:** In Ukraine, giving birth now means going underground first.

*Confirmed: this stays in full voice. The danger here is being actively managed around, not inflicted on the newborns — the hospitals' entire effort is protective. That's the distinction the exception trigger runs on now (see §4).*

### Plain register (exception triggered) — Lindsay Clancy trial
**Headline:** Lindsay Clancy Jury Remains Deadlocked
**What Happened:** Jurors in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy, accused of killing her three children in 2023, remain deadlocked after six days of deliberation. The panel is reportedly split 11 to 1. Deliberations resume Friday.
**Why It Matters:** Clancy's defense argues she was experiencing postpartum psychosis at the time of the deaths; prosecutors argue she is criminally responsible. A mistrial doesn't answer the question everyone wanted answered — it just ends the asking, for now. Cases like this test something specific: whether a system built to deliver verdicts can actually resolve situations that were never going to fit cleanly into one.
**What's Next:** The jury resumes deliberations Friday morning. A third deadlock notice would likely lead to a mistrial.
**Takeaway:** After six days, the jury still hasn't decided whether Clancy is responsible for her children's deaths.

Notice what's absent compared to the full-voice examples: no fragment-for-rhythm, no aphoristic turn, no line built to be screenshotted on its own strength. The insight is there — it just isn't performing.

### Tribute register — a drag icon's obituary
**Headline:** Kansas City Drag Icon Melinda Ryder Dies At 70
**What Happened:** Melinda Ryder, a fixture of Kansas City's drag and LGBTQ nightlife scene for five decades, has died at 70. She helped build Hamburger Mary's into a home for the city's drag community long before it had one anywhere else.
**Why It Matters:** A community's history often lives in one person's calendar of shows long after the venues and the era around them have changed. When that person is gone, so is the only complete record of how a scene actually survived — who showed up on the slow nights, what got built before anyone thought to write it down. Ryder was that record for Kansas City.
**What's Next:** A memorial performance is expected at Hamburger Mary's in the coming weeks.
**Takeaway:** Fifty years of Tuesday nights built a community that outlived the fear it started in.

*This stays in the Tribute Register: natural death, no violence, no active proceeding — the reflective, resonant closing line here is the goal, not a risk, unlike the Clancy example above.*

## 7. Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] All 6 fields populated
- [ ] Headline is Title Case and fits one line on mobile
- [ ] Why It Matters is 40-80 words (100 only if genuinely needed) and does all three jobs: impact, discourse shape (if relevant), pattern-level insight
- [ ] Discourse-awareness check: if public reaction is mentioned, does it note shape without characterizing who's right?
- [ ] Swap test run on the philosophical line: still true with different names in it?
- [ ] "What's next" is grounded in a real, stated next step (or says there isn't one)
- [ ] Exception trigger checked: death, violence, or child harm — involving anyone, named or not? If yes, determine register: **Tribute Register** for natural death/terminal illness/grief-tribute (warmth and resonance welcome), **strict plain register** for violent death or death tied to an active proceeding (insight stays, ornamentation goes)
- [ ] Mandatory human review checked: does this involve a named individual acquitted or cleared in a sexual-abuse-adjacent case? If yes, this holds for review regardless of register — no auto-publish
- [ ] Art selection rationale written and defensible to someone who didn't pick it
- [ ] Superlatives/adjectives checked against the verifiable-vs-verdict test
- [ ] Read aloud test: does it sound like the voice you intended for this register, not a generic wire-copy default?

## Open Items for the Team
- Whether "child harm" needs a more specific definition, now that it's carrying more weight as one of only three trigger categories, and now that it explicitly excludes protective/risk-mitigation stories (see §4)
- The ornamentation-vs-insight line for trigger-category philosophical takeaways is new and untested at volume — worth revisiting after a few real weeks of drafting to see where it actually gets hard to judge
- The Tribute Register is brand new and only tested against one real case (the drag icon obituary) — worth watching for edge cases where "natural death" and "violent/disputed death" aren't cleanly separable (e.g., a death from long-term illness that was itself caused by an accident or assault years earlier)
- The mandatory-review category (named acquittals in sexual-abuse-adjacent cases) may need to expand — worth tracking whether other categories of reputational-risk-regardless-of-register cases surface as the pipeline runs against more real days of news
